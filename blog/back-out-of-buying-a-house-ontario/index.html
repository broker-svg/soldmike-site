Can You Back Out of Buying a House in Ontario?

November 28, 2026 · Michael Barillari, Broker

Buying a home is a big decision, and it's normal to wonder: "What if I change my mind?" Maybe the inspection worries you, the financing gets tight, or you simply get cold feet.

The short answer: not once the deal is firm. Resale homes in Ontario have no cooling-off period. Your conditions (financing, inspection, status certificate) are your way out, and only until their deadlines. New condos bought from a builder are the exception, with a 10-day cooling-off period.

Here's what that means in practice.

Resale: no cooling-off period

When you buy a resale home in Ontario, there's no window after signing to change your mind. Once your offer is accepted and your conditions are cleared, the deal is firm and you're committed.

That's why the time to protect yourself is before the deal goes firm, not after.

Conditions: your way out

Conditions are terms in your offer that must be met before the deal becomes firm. The common ones:

  • Financing. Your lender confirms your mortgage.
  • Home inspection. You inspect the home and review the results.
  • Status certificate (condos). Your lawyer reviews the condo corporation's package.

Each condition has a deadline. Until that deadline, the condition is your way out. Once you waive your conditions, or the deadlines pass, the deal is firm.

Conditions protect you, but they make your offer weaker. When a seller compares offers, they look at price, deposit, conditions and closing date, not just the top number.

Going in firm on an offer night

When a seller sets an offer date, buyers compete by coming in with a strong price, a solid deposit and as few conditions as possible. Many buyers sort out their financing and inspection before the offer date so they can make a firm offer.

That can win you the home. It also means you have no conditions to fall back on. If you go in firm, be sure you can close. In particular, know your financing will hold. The mortgage stress test qualifies you at a higher rate than the one you'll pay, so get pre-approved before you start looking. More on how offer nights work in our offer nights post.

Your deposit

Your deposit shows the seller you're serious. In the GTA, about 5% of the price is common, usually due within 24 hours of your offer being accepted. It's held in trust by the listing brokerage and counts toward your down payment on closing.

Have it ready before you offer. Once your offer is accepted, the clock starts right away. If you're ever thinking about walking away from a deal, talk to your lawyer first.

Condos: the status certificate

For a resale condo, the status certificate is one of your key protections. It's a package from the condo corporation showing its finances, reserve fund, rules and any lawsuits. Buyers usually make their offer conditional on their lawyer reviewing it.

If your lawyer finds something that concerns you, that condition is your chance to act, before its deadline. Our condo buyer checklist covers what else to look at.

New condos from a builder: the exception

New condos bought from a builder come with a 10-day cooling-off period. That's the one main exception to the "firm means firm" rule.

Pre-construction is different in other ways too. It spreads out the deposit and gets you a brand-new home, but closings can be delayed for years and you may pay extra development charges at closing. Our pre-construction or resale post compares the two.

What this means for you

  • Resale has no cooling-off period. Firm means firm.
  • Your conditions are your way out, and only until their deadlines.
  • Get pre-approved first so your financing is solid.
  • Inspect before an offer date if you plan to go in firm.
  • Buying a condo? Make your offer conditional on your lawyer reviewing the status certificate.
  • New builder condos come with a 10-day cooling-off period.
  • Read it all, then sign.
