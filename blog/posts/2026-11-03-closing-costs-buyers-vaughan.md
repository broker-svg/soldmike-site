Most buyers have their down payment worked out to the dollar. The cheque that surprises them is the one that comes after: closing costs.

**The short answer:** plan for about **2% of the purchase price** on top of your down payment. Most of that is **Ontario land transfer tax**. On a **$1.3 million home in Vaughan**, the land transfer tax alone is about **$22,500**.

Here's what goes into that 2%, how the tax is worked out, and what changes if you're a first-time buyer or buying in Toronto.

## What the 2% covers

Closing costs are everything you pay to close the deal that isn't the price of the home itself. For a resale home in Vaughan, that usually means:

- **Ontario land transfer tax.** The biggest piece by far.
- **Legal fees.** Your real estate lawyer handles the title transfer, the mortgage and the closing funds.
- **Title insurance.** Usually arranged through your lawyer.
- **A home inspection.** In the GTA it usually costs **$400 to $700** and takes two to three hours.
- **Adjustments.** If the seller has already prepaid property tax for part of the year you'll own the home, you pay them back for those days on closing.

On a $1.3 million home, 2% is about **$26,000**, on top of your down payment.

## How Ontario land transfer tax is worked out

Land transfer tax is charged in brackets, a bit like income tax. Each slice of the price is taxed at its own rate:

- **0.5%** on the first **$55,000**
- **1%** on the portion from $55,000 to **$250,000**
- **1.5%** on the portion from $250,000 to **$400,000**
- **2%** on the portion from $400,000 to **$2 million**

The first $400,000 of any price always works out to **$4,475** in tax. After that, every dollar up to $2 million is taxed at 2%.

## What it looks like at Vaughan prices

Using those brackets:

- **$1,000,000 home:** $4,475 plus 2% of $600,000 ($12,000) = about **$16,475**.
- **$1,300,000 home:** $4,475 plus 2% of $900,000 ($18,000) = about **$22,500**.
- **$1,500,000 home:** $4,475 plus 2% of $1,100,000 ($22,000) = about **$26,475**.

That's why land transfer tax is most of your closing costs. Legal fees, title insurance, the inspection and adjustments make up the rest.

## Buying in Toronto? It roughly doubles

If the home is inside the City of Toronto, you also pay a **municipal land transfer tax** of about the same amount as the provincial one. So a buyer moving from Vaughan into Toronto at the same price can expect roughly **double the land transfer tax**.

If you're comparing a home in Woodbridge or Maple with one just south of Steeles, put this in the math. It can change which home is really the better deal.

## First-time buyers get money back

If you've never owned a home, you may qualify for a refund:

- **Ontario** refunds up to **$4,000** of the provincial land transfer tax.
- **The City of Toronto** refunds up to **$4,475** of its municipal tax.
- So a first-time buyer in Toronto can save up to **$8,475**.

To qualify, you must **never have owned a home anywhere in the world**, and you must **move in within nine months**. Your lawyer claims the refund for you at closing, so tell them early that you're a first-time buyer.

On a $1,000,000 home in Vaughan, the $4,000 refund takes the provincial tax from about $16,475 down to about $12,475.

## Other money to have ready

Closing costs aren't the only cash you need besides the down payment:

- **Your deposit.** In the GTA, about **5% of the price**, usually due **within 24 hours** of your offer being accepted. It's held in trust by the listing brokerage and counts toward your down payment, but it has to be ready to move right away.
- **PST on mortgage insurance.** If you put down less than 20%, in Ontario the provincial sales tax on the insurance premium can't be added to your mortgage. You pay it in cash when you get your mortgage.
- **Pre-construction buyers** may pay extra development charges at closing. Read our [pre-construction page](/buy/pre-construction/) before you sign with a builder.

## What this means for you

- **Budget 2% on top of your down payment** as a starting point.
- **Work out your land transfer tax** at the price you actually plan to pay, using the brackets above.
- **Add Toronto's tax** if you're looking inside the city.
- **Tell your lawyer you're a first-time buyer** so they claim the refund at closing.
- **Keep the cash liquid.** Your deposit is due within a day, and closing costs are due on closing day.
