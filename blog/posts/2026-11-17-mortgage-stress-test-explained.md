You've picked a lender, you know your rate and you've worked out a payment you can live with. Then the lender tells you that you qualify for less than you expected. Often, that's the stress test.

**The short answer:** lenders check that you could still afford your mortgage payments at a **higher rate** than the one you're actually getting. Right now that test rate is the **greater of 5.25% or your mortgage rate plus 2%**. It lowers how much you can borrow, so get pre-approved before you start looking.

Here's how it works, and what it means for your search.

## What the stress test actually does

The stress test doesn't change your mortgage. It changes your approval.

- **Your payment** is based on the rate you actually get.
- **Your approval** is based on a higher test rate.
- The lender checks whether you could still carry the payments at that test rate. If you could, you qualify for that amount. If you couldn't, the amount you can borrow comes down.

You never pay the test rate. It's a safety margin built into the approval, so that if rates rise or money gets tight, you can still make your payments.

## How the test rate is set

The lender takes the **greater** of two numbers:

- **5.25%**, or
- **your mortgage rate plus 2%**.

Two quick illustrations of how the rule works (these are examples, not rate forecasts):

- If you're offered **3%**, your rate plus 2% is 5%. That's below 5.25%, so you're tested at **5.25%**.
- If you're offered **4%**, your rate plus 2% is 6%. That's above 5.25%, so you're tested at **6%**.

So the 5.25% floor decides the test when rates are lower, and the "plus 2%" decides it when rates are higher. Either way, you're qualified at a rate above the one you'll pay.

## Why it matters when you shop

The stress test sets **how much you can borrow**. Your down payment sets **how much you need to bring**. You need both numbers before you know your real price range.

A quick reminder on down payments in Ontario:

- At least **5% of the first $500,000**.
- Plus **10% of the portion between $500,000 and $1.5 million**.
- Homes at **$1.5 million or more need 20% down**.

We walked through those tiers at real Vaughan prices in our [down payment guide](/blog/down-payment-ontario/).

Then plan for the money that comes due before and at closing:

- **Closing costs of about 2%** of the price. Most of it is land transfer tax, about **$22,500 on a $1.3 million home in Vaughan**. Our [closing costs post](/blog/closing-costs-buyers-vaughan/) breaks it down.
- **A deposit of about 5%** of the price, usually due **within 24 hours** of your offer being accepted. It's held in trust and counts toward your down payment on closing.

## Get pre-approved first

Getting pre-approved is step three in our [buyer guide](/buy/buyer-guide/), and for good reason. A lender confirms what you can borrow, with the stress test already built in, and holds a rate. That gives you three things:

- **A real price range**, so you don't fall for a home you can't finance.
- **Stronger offers.** Sellers take pre-approved buyers more seriously.
- **Less risk on a firm deal.** Resale homes in Ontario have **no cooling-off period**. Your financing condition is your way out, and only until its deadline. If you go in firm, for example on an offer night, you need to know your financing will hold.

## Selling to buy?

If you're moving up, the money from your sale often funds your next down payment. That's one reason we suggest selling first in most cases, or lining up both closings for the same day. If you buy first, ask your lender about a bridge loan to cover the gap.

To see what your sale leaves you with, try our [cost to sell calculator](/sell/cost-to-sell-vaughan/). It covers commission and HST, legal fees and your mortgage payout.

## What this means for you

- **Expect to qualify at a higher rate** than the one you'll pay. That's normal.
- **Know your test rate:** the greater of 5.25% or your rate plus 2%.
- **Get pre-approved before you look,** not after you find the house.
- **Budget for your down payment plus about 2% closing costs,** with a deposit of about 5% ready to move when your offer is accepted.
- **Don't go firm** unless your financing is sorted.
