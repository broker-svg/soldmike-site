Owning a home with tenants in it and deciding to sell raises a lot of questions fast. Can you even list it? Do the tenants have to leave? Who tells them, and when?

**The short answer:** yes, you can sell a house with tenants in it. The tenancy carries on with the new owner **unless the buyer, or a close family member, will move in**. In that case the tenant gets an **N12 notice** with **at least 60 days**, ending on the last day of a rental period, and **one month's rent** as compensation.

The rules are the easy part. The timing is where sellers get stuck, so let's walk through both.

## What happens to the lease when you sell

Selling the house doesn't end the tenancy on its own. If the buyer is an investor who wants to keep renting it out, the tenant simply stays and the new owner becomes their landlord.

That opens up two kinds of buyers:

- **Investors** who want a home that's already rented.
- **Buyers who want to live in it** themselves, or have a close family member live in it.

Which group you're selling to shapes almost every other decision, from the price to the closing date.

## When an N12 notice comes in

If the buyer, or a close family member, plans to move in, the tenant gets an N12 notice. Three things to know:

- **At least 60 days' notice.** The tenant gets at least 60 days.
- **It ends on the last day of a rental period.** If rent is paid monthly, that's usually the last day of a month.
- **One month's rent as compensation.** The tenant is owed one month's rent.

Put those together and you can see why the closing date matters so much. A closing date that doesn't leave room for the full notice period can turn into a problem for everyone. Get your lawyer involved early, before you agree to a date.

## Sell with tenants in, or wait until they leave?

Both can work. It depends on your home, your tenants and your timeline.

**Selling with tenants in place** can suit an investor buyer, and you keep collecting rent while the home is on the market. The tradeoff is less control over how the home looks for photos and showings.

**Having the tenants move out first** gives you a vacant home you can clean, prepare and stage before the photos. That's what we did at **2 La Maria Lane in Maple**. The home was tenant-occupied, which can make a sale slow and stressful for an owner. We guided the sellers through having the tenants move out properly, then got the home cleaned up, prepared and staged before the photos and launch. The sellers were very happy with how smooth the process was.

## Don't forget the money side

A rented home is often a rental or investment property, and that changes the tax picture. The principal residence exemption generally covers the gain on the home you live in, but **investment and rental properties are taxable**. Talk to your accountant before you sell.

## What this means for you

- **You can sell with tenants in.** The lease carries on with the new owner unless the buyer or a close family member moves in.
- **If they move in,** the tenant gets an N12 with at least 60 days, ending on the last day of a rental period, plus one month's rent.
- **Plan the timing before you list,** so your closing date fits the notice period.
- **Decide early** whether you're selling to an investor or to someone who will live there.
- **Talk to your accountant** if the home has been a rental.
