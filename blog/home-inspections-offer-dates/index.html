Home Inspections: Cost, Timing and Offer Dates

November 24, 2026 · Michael Barillari, Broker

You've found the home. The layout works, the street feels right, and you can already picture the kids' rooms. Then comes the question every buyer asks: "Do I need an inspection, and when do I fit it in?"

The short answer: yes, for almost every resale home. An inspection usually costs $400 to $700 in the GTA and takes two to three hours. When there's an offer date, buyers often inspect before it so they can make a firm offer.

Here's how it works, and how to plan it around your offer.

Why an inspection matters

A showing tells you if the home works for you. An inspection helps you understand what you're actually buying.

When you tour, look past the staging at layout, light, roof, windows and the furnace age. Small signs can point to big costs. An inspection gives you a closer look before you commit.

It matters even more in Ontario because resale homes have no cooling-off period. Once your deal is firm, you're committed. If something worries you, you want to know before that point, not after.

What it costs

Plan for about $400 to $700 in the GTA. It's one of the items in your closing budget. Buyers should plan for about 2% of the purchase price on top of the down payment, which covers land transfer tax, legal fees, title insurance, a home inspection and adjustments. Our closing costs post walks through the full list.

Timing: two ways to do it

How you fit the inspection in depends on how the home is being sold.

1. As a condition of your offer

When there's no offer date, or the seller takes offers any time, you can make your offer conditional on a home inspection. Your other common conditions are financing and, for condos, a status certificate.

  • Each condition has a deadline.
  • You book the inspection and review the results before that deadline.
  • Once your conditions are waived, the deal is firm.

Conditions protect you, but they make your offer weaker. A seller comparing offers looks at price, deposit, conditions and closing date, not just the top number.

2. Before the offer date

When a seller sets an offer date, every buyer sends their best offer by that date. The seller can accept, sign back or turn down any of them.

To compete, buyers come in with a strong price, a solid deposit and as few conditions as possible, with financing and inspection sorted before the offer date. That's why many buyers inspect ahead of time. It lets you go in firm, knowing what you're buying.

The offer date is usually about a week after listing, so there's a window. Book early in that week. More on how offer nights work in our offer nights post.

If you're selling

Inspections matter on your side too. After you accept an offer, the buyer's financing and inspection conditions need to come off before the deal goes firm. That's one reason we prepare a short list of repairs, paint and decluttering before you list. Our low-cost fixes post covers the simple ones.

When we review offers with you, we compare every one on price, deposit, conditions and closing. An offer with an inspection condition and a deadline isn't the same as a firm offer at the same price.

Don't forget the rest of the timeline

The inspection is one step in a longer list:

  • Get pre-approved before you start looking.
  • Have your deposit ready. In the GTA about 5% of the price is common, usually due within 24 hours of acceptance.
  • Clear your conditions before their deadlines.
  • Final walkthrough before closing, to confirm everything is as agreed.

Our how long to buy post lays out the full timeline.

What this means for you

  • Get an inspection on almost every resale home.
  • Budget $400 to $700 and two to three hours.
  • With an offer date, inspect before it if you want to go in firm.
  • Without one, use an inspection condition and watch the deadline.
  • Remember there's no cooling-off period on resale. Know what you're buying before the deal is firm.
